11. Methodological Limitations
  1. Complaint metadata is synthetic and generated for academic supervised-learning experimentation.
  2. ATM historical activity is synthetic and reflects simulated operational baselines, not real bank transaction records.
  3. OpenStreetMap ATM coverage is crowdsourced and incomplete (e.g. Panipat catalog has 0 OSM ATMs).
  4. Bank/operator metadata may be unrecorded in public OSM records, receiving default neutral compatibility scores.
  5. Candidate ATM ranking is an analytical prioritization tool, not forensic proof of criminal withdrawal.
  6. Zone boundaries are statistical spatial bounding representations derived from training centroids.
  7. Model predictions depend on training data distribution and may reflect regional class frequency imbalance.
  8. Confidential banking switch records and CCTV footage were not accessed by CyberTrace.
  9. Authoritative cardholder audit logs and device telecom tower records are required for legal corroboration.
  10. Prediction uncertainty exists in boundary regions (e.g. NCR sectors), requiring multi-sector evaluation.
12. Recommended Investigative Verification Steps
  • Issue formal legal notice to the acquiring bank for ATM electronic journal (EJ) logs and cash-out timestamps.
  • Request surveillance CCTV footage for top candidate ATMs covering a ±30 minute window around incident timing.
  • Cross-reference suspect cellular tower Call Detail Records (CDR) against candidate ATM geographic coordinates.
  • Request National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) interchange switch logs for transaction tracing.
  • Verify physical operational status and cash replenishment records for candidate ATMs during the incident window.
  • File official intelligence documentation under the victim's National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal reference.
